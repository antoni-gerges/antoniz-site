Antoni Gerges

  1. Al Forsan International Sports Resort · Abu Dhabi · 2009

    Al Forsan means the knights. I began with them.

    A resort for riding, motor sport, shooting, paintball and water sport needed a brand before it opened. Before any shape, we read the history of Arab horsemen.

    From the brand manual, 2009.

  2. Getting inspired

    A gateway, a tent, a helmet: the knight’s place.

    The arch came from the resort’s own gateway and the peak of a Bedouin tent. The dome came from a knight’s helmet.

  3. The mark

    An arch to ride through.

    The dunes first, then the arch rising out of them, the dome, and a rider coming through. Gold for the desert, near-black for the rider.

    The manual’s own vectors, animated for this portfolio

II

One system

A resort of five sports and five restaurants, and one family of signs for all of them.

  1. A kit of parts

    Every piece can work alone.

    The rider, the arch, the dune line: each one carries the brand on its own, on a sign, a ticket or a flag.

    Brand language, from the manual.

  2. Brand architecture

    One arch, five sports, a colour each.

    The same mark, the same grid, the same space around it. Only the colour of the centre’s name changes.

    1. Equestrian#005596
    2. Motor sports#ef3e42
    3. Shooting#008a5f
    4. Paintball#fcb568
    5. Water sports#13b5ea

    Colours read from the manual’s own palette pages, 121 to 133.

  3. Pictures

    Chosen for speed, and for class.

    Two moods for every picture the resort would use: hyperactive and fast, or corporate and classic.

  4. The manual

    136 pages, so the resort could run on it.

    Mark, grid, colour, pictures, voice, stationery, advertising, signage, the five centres. Approved in March 2009.

    Eight of the 136 pages.

III

Your gateway to awesomeness

Then I ran the brand as the resort’s agency. The arch became a gate that every sport bursts through.

  1. The key visual

    Every sport, out of one gate.

    A rider, a shooter, a paintballer, a kart and a wakeboarder, all moving at once.

  2. In print

    Then the gate itself, with the sports riding through it.

    The master, then one advertisement for each centre.

    Press advertisements, from 2009. Shown above their bottom band.

  3. The line

    Where knights rise.

    The same words on advertising, billboards and the resort’s own screens.

    From the manual’s advertising and signage pages.

IV

In the desert

Then the brand stood in the sand, around the resort as it was being built.

  1. On site

    Hoardings along the road, as the resort was built.

    I put the campaign around the site itself: riders, karts and shooters running for hundreds of metres.

    Photographs of the hoardings on site. Undated in the record.

  2. Signs and things

    To the sport resort. To the paintball field.

    Signs in the centre’s colour, and the key visual on the back of a T-shirt.

    Signage from the manual; T-shirt from the exhibition.

The record

I built the Al Forsan brand from its first research: the mark, the brand across its five sports and its restaurants, and a 136-page manual. Then I ran it as the resort’s agency.

For
Al Forsan International Sports Resort, Abu Dhabi, through its owner group PPM (Private Property Management)
When
Identity approved December 2008 to March 2009; the manual re-delivered in February 2012; campaign, signs, hoardings and exhibition work from 2009 to 2013
Where
Abu Dhabi
My part
Creative Director and Producer, through AntBrush, my studio: the research, the mark, the brand architecture, the manual, then the campaign “Your gateway to awesomeness”, the site hoardings, signage and the exhibition stand.
Made
The mark; five sport-centre versions (equestrian, motor sports, shooting, paintball, water sports) and marks for five restaurants; a 136-page manual; stationery; press advertisements; site hoardings; signage; an exhibition stand; posters and T-shirts.
Not claimed
The dates of the hoarding and stand photographs, and the year of the exhibition, are not in the record.

Sources: the brand identity guidelines (136 pages); the case boards and photographs in the archive; the client’s correspondence of February 2009. No money figures appear here. Private preview. Nothing here is published.
